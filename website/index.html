Opening it the first time

Your computer may double-check. That's normal.

Magic Paint is a small independent app, so Mac and Windows show a one-time safety message. Here's how to get past it.

On a Mac

  1. Open MagicPaint-Mac.dmg and drag Magic Paint into Applications.
  2. Open it. If you see "Apple could not verify…", click Done.
  3. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.
  4. Confirm once. After that, it opens normally.

On Windows

  1. Run MagicPaint-Windows-Setup.exe.
  2. If "Windows protected your PC" appears, click More info.
  3. Click Run anyway. It installs in a few seconds, no admin needed.
  4. Find Magic Paint on your desktop and in the Start menu.

Questions

Good questions from parents.

Is it really free?

Yes. Magic Paint is free to download and use, with no ads, no subscriptions and no in-app purchases.

What ages is it for?

It's designed for ages 3 to 8. Younger kids love the stickers and the paint bucket, and older kids love the kaleidoscope and the coloring pages.

Does it need the internet?

No. Once it's installed, everything (brushes, coloring pages, Drip's voice) works offline. The browser version needs internet only to load the first time.

Where do saved paintings go?

When your kid presses Save, the desktop app asks where to save the picture and suggests your Pictures folder. It saves a regular PNG image you can print or share.

Why does my computer show a warning?

Apple and Microsoft show a one-time message for apps from small independent makers that haven't paid for a developer certificate. The steps above get you past it. Magic Paint doesn't collect data, connect to the internet or change your system.

Does it work on iPad or Chromebook?

Yes. Use Play online. On an iPad, tap Share → Add to Home Screen so it opens like an app.
